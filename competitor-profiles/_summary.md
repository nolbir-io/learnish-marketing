## The core framing: two competitions, not one

Learnish competes on **two different axes** at once (Phase 0 decision):

1. **Attention** — competing for the *child's time*. The enemy is the screen and the scroll: **YouTube/YouTube Kids**, TikTok, Instagram, Netflix, Roblox. This frames the *problem* and the emotional tension (screen-time guilt, safety anxiety).
2. **Category** — competing for the *parent's learning spend*. The alternatives: free learning apps (**Khan Academy Kids**, **Duolingo**), STEAM kits (**KiwiCo**, **MEL Science**), in-person (**local tutoring centers & kids clubs**, **Outschool**), and à-la-carte STEAM toys on local marketplaces (**Uzum**).

Learnish's whole strategy is to use **category quality** (real learning, safe, structured) to win the **attention battle** parents feel guilty about losing to screens.

## Where Learnish wins (the through-line)

No single competitor does all three of: **(1) reviewed, safe, age-appropriate content · (2) a physical kit in the child's hands · (3) a local, in-person, trusted community.** Each rival is strong on one axis and structurally weak on the others:

- Global apps (Khan, Duolingo) own digital but **can't be local or physical or in-person**.
- Global kits (KiwiCo, MEL) own the box but **can't ship cheap/fast locally or build community**.
- Local centers own in-person trust but **are fragmented, offline-only, single-subject, unbranded**.
- YouTube owns attention but **isn't learning, isn't safe-by-design, isn't trusted by parents**.

**Learnish's moat = local in-person trust + community** bundled with a generous digital layer and a local kit operation — the one combination global attention apps and global EdTech structurally cannot replicate here. (See [operating principles](../docs/strategy/operating-principles.md).)

## Strategic takeaways

1. **Don't out-free the free.** The app is top-of-funnel (fuels signups); revenue comes from kits + workshops + Club, not from gating the app.
2. **Borrow the habit mechanic** (Duolingo streak / "Nish" the bee) — match the one thing that makes digital sticky. (OQ-004.)
3. **Localize as a weapon** — Uzbek-first content, local delivery, local payment/BNPL beat every global player on fit.
4. **Turn the local incumbent into distribution** — tutoring centers/venues become workshop hosts (30% revenue share), not just rivals.
5. **Sell safety + community, not features** — the emotional wedge vs. YouTube and the trust wedge vs. fragmented local centers are the same promise: *safe, trusted, and together.*
