### LI-2026-06-01 · Long-form · Draft · Thesis
Publish: 2026-06-09
Campaign: Phase 1 — First 10 trusted families

**Hook:**
Uzbekistan has 36M+ people, one of the youngest populations in the region, and parents who badly want their kids to do something better with their time than scroll.

**Body:**
Two competitions are happening for every kid here.

The first is for their **attention** — YouTube, TikTok, Roblox, Netflix. Free, infinite, and a quiet source of guilt for almost every parent.

The second is for the **spend** — global EdTech apps (Khan Academy Kids, Duolingo), kit subscriptions (KiwiCo-style), workshops (Outschool-style), and local tutoring centers and kids clubs.

The global players win attention and have polished apps. But there's one thing none of them can replicate at scale in Uzbekistan: **local, in-person trust.** A vetted teacher your child sees on Saturday, in your district, with you in the room.

That's the wedge Learnish is built on. We start with safe, hands-on weekend workshops — reviewed, age-appropriate, run by local teachers — then wrap them with an app, courses, and STEAM kits into one membership: the Learnish Club. The workshop earns the trust; the Club keeps the family.

Think LEGO's craft, Duolingo's habit, and Disney's family trust — built locally, where a global app can't follow.

If you invest in consumer or EdTech in emerging markets, or in Central Asia specifically, I'd welcome a conversation.

### LI-2026-06-02 · Long-form · Draft · Why now
Publish: 2026-06-16
Campaign: Phase 1 — First 10 trusted families

**Hook:**
Three things had to be true at once for a community-led kids' learning company in Uzbekistan to work. They are, now.

**Body:**
**1. The screen-time tension is now a daily parenting problem.**
Smartphones are everywhere, kids are online younger, and parents feel the guilt without a good alternative. The demand for "something better for my child to do" is real and growing.

**2. Local supply exists but is fragmented.**
There are good local teachers, tutoring centers, and kids clubs — but no trusted brand that bundles safe, reviewed, hands-on learning across workshops, kits, and courses. Parents stitch together scattered apps, toys, and tutoring themselves.

**3. The trust gap is structural, not temporary.**
Global attention apps optimize for time-on-screen, not a parent's peace of mind. Global EdTech apps don't run a Saturday workshop in your district. That gap — local, in-person, reviewed-and-age-appropriate trust — is exactly what a community-led model can own.

A workshop-led flywheel turns each session into signups, referrals, and the next workshop. It's slower than paid acquisition and far more durable — because it compounds on trust the incumbents can't manufacture here.

That's the wedge. That's why now.

### LI-2026-06-03 · Long-form · Draft · The model (partners + ambassadors)
Publish: 2026-06-23
Campaign: Phase 1 — First 10 trusted families

**Hook:**
The hardest part of a kids' learning business isn't content. It's earning a parent's trust — and doing it at a cost that scales.

**Body:**
Our answer is a community model with two kinds of local partners.

**Venue partners.** Local centers and kids clubs already own the trust and the space we need. Instead of competing with them, we turn them into **workshop venues** — they host, we bring the playbook and the teacher, and revenue splits across teacher, venue, and platform. The rival becomes the channel.

**Student ambassadors.** Ambassadors run on-the-ground workshop ops — sourcing vetted local teachers, filling sessions, signing families up at the door. They're comped with membership, store credits, and real experience, not payroll. That keeps the model lean and doubles as a community and acquisition engine.

The founder runs the first lighthouse workshops personally. The test for the next phase is simple and brutal: **can a session run, fill, and convert families without the founder in the room?** If yes, the model franchises district by district.

We're recruiting our first venue partners and student ambassadors in Tashkent now.

**CTA:** If you run a kids club or center, or you're a student who wants to build something real in your district — DM me.

### LI-2026-06-04 · Long-form · Draft · Category framing

Campaign: Phase 1 — First 10 trusted families

**Hook:**
People keep asking which category Learnish is in. The honest answer is that the interesting part is between the categories.

**Body:**
**It's not a YouTube replacement.** We're not competing for infinite passive attention. We're offering the thing parents actually want instead of it: something safe their child *makes*.

**It's not a global EdTech app.** Khan Academy Kids and Duolingo are excellent — and they don't run a reviewed, age-appropriate workshop in your district on Saturday with a vetted teacher and you in the room. The app is our on-ramp, not our moat.

**It's not a single tutoring center.** One center serves one neighborhood. We're a brand and a playbook that lets many local teachers and venues run to the same safe standard — and bundles workshops, kits, and courses into one membership.

**It's not a toy store.** The STEAM kits matter, but they're one layer of a learning relationship with a family, not a transaction.

Learnish is the trusted weekend-learning brand for kids 5–17, built community-first. The category that looks "new" is usually an old one missing a piece. The missing piece here is **local trust, productized.**

### LI-2026-06-05 · Long-form · Draft · The metric we optimize
Publish: 2026-07-07
Campaign: Phase 1 — First 10 trusted families

**Hook:**
Most consumer startups optimize for signups. We watch two numbers: **free family signups** (our north-star) and **paying Club families** (the gate on each phase).

**Body:**
Signups tell us the top of the flywheel is turning — parents are coming to workshops and creating free family accounts. Paying Club families tell us the trust converted into a real, recurring relationship.

Why both, and in that order: in kids' learning the failure mode isn't getting attention — it's converting attention into trust, and trust into retention. Plenty of apps get downloads no one uses. We'd rather earn 10 families who'd refer us than 10,000 who forget us.

So our milestones gate on **paying families** — 10, then 100, then 1,000 — while free signups scale several times faster in the background. Every roadmap, hiring, and budget decision is gated on whether the workshop → free signup → Club → referral loop is actually compounding.

For investors who back community-led, trust-first consumer businesses in emerging markets: I'd welcome conversations with funds and angels who think in compounding loops, not paid-growth funnels.
