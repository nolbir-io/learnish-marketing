# Pitch deck — Venue & community partners (host a Learnish workshop)

**Version:** v1 (draft)
**Purpose:** Recruit local centers, kids clubs, schools, libraries, and community spaces in Tashkent to **host Learnish weekend workshops** as venue partners. Replaces the archived Own marketplace-escape sales deck — Learnish is B2C and community-led, so the one "sales-style" motion that matters is **partner recruitment**: turning the local centers that own community trust into venues (the model's 30% venue share), per the Phase 5 decision *"local centers: compete AND partner."*
**Primary audience:** Owners/managers of local kids centers, tutoring centers, clubs, schools, and community venues in Tashkent districts.
**Secondary use:** Adaptable for **ambassador/teacher recruitment** (campus talks) and as a leave-behind for community organizers.
**Sources:**
- `.agents/product-marketing-context.md` (positioning, audience, glossary)
- `pricing/monetization-model.md` (workshop marketplace split, P&L)
- `docs/playbook/phase-1-first-10.md` + `phase-2-first-100.md` (the flywheel, why venues matter)
- `competitor-profiles/local-tutoring-and-clubs.md` (compete-AND-partner frame)
- `brand-voice/voice-guide.md` (ambassador/partner tone: "trusted collaborator, mission-driven")

> **Honesty constraints (non-negotiable):** No fabricated attendance numbers, family counts, testimonials, or earnings claims. Every figure is either a **structural fact** (the 40/30/30 revenue split) or a clearly-marked **`[PROOF — to collect]`** placeholder until real session data exists. The revenue-share % is the model; per-session earnings depend on real ticket price and fill (`pricing/monetization-model.md`).

## Slide 1 — Cover

**On-slide:**
> **Learnish**
> Host a safe, exciting weekend workshop for kids in your community.
>
> *We bring the teachers and the projects. You bring the space and the families.*

**Visual:** Warm photo of kids mid-build at a real session in a local venue. Learnish wordmark. Nish (the bee) as a small, friendly accent. Local, not corporate.

**Speaker notes:** Open with the venue's name and what they already do. *"You already run a great space for kids in [district]. I want to show you how a Learnish weekend could add to it — at no cost and little effort on your side."*

---

## Slide 2 — The opportunity in your community

**Headline:** Parents want a safe, exciting weekend for their kids — and they don't have one.

**Body (3 short points):**
- Parents feel the screen-time tension and want hands-on, real-world learning.
- Trustworthy, age-appropriate options are scattered and hard to vet.
- The parents who already trust *your* space are exactly the ones asking for this.

**Speaker notes:** The demand is local and real. The question isn't whether parents want it — it's who they'll trust to provide it. That trust is what you already have.

---

## Slide 3 — What a Learnish workshop is

**Headline:** A real project. A vetted teacher. A great Saturday.

**Body:** A hands-on weekend session (Sat/Sun) where kids 5–17 build something real and take it home — robotics, science, art, code, and more. Run by a vetted local teacher. Parents welcome to stay. `[PROOF — list 3–5 real workshop topics + age bands.]`

**On-slide facts:** 20–30 kids per session · weekends · age-grouped · reviewed, age-appropriate projects.

**Speaker notes:** Walk through one concrete example session. Concrete beats abstract — name the project and what the kid takes home.

---

## Slide 4 — You already have the two things that matter

**Headline:** Space and community. That's the whole asset.

**Two columns:**
| Your space | Your community |
|---|---|
| A safe, known room where local kids already come. No new venue for parents to learn to trust. | The families who trust your center are the first to say yes — and to bring friends. |

**Closing line:** Learnish is the easiest weekend program you'll ever add — because you already have the hard parts.

**Speaker notes:** This reframes the venue from "a rival tutoring center" to "the partner who owns the trust." (Phase 5: compete AND partner.)

---

## Slide 5 — How the partnership works

**Headline:** Simple roles. We carry the load.

**Body (who does what):**
| Learnish brings | You bring |
|---|---|
| Vetted teacher, reviewed curriculum, the project kits/materials, booking + marketing (we fill the seats), on-the-ground ambassador to run logistics, safety standards | The space on a weekend, and a warm introduction to the families who already come to your center |

**Speaker notes:** Make it obvious this is low-effort for them. They host; we operate.

---

## Slide 6 — What you earn (and how)

**Headline:** A share of every seat. Structural, not a promise.

**Body — the model (structural fact):**
> Each workshop ticket is split: **Platform 40% · Teacher 30% · Venue 30%.**
> As the host venue, you earn **30% of every seat sold** at your location.

**Illustrative only (mark clearly):**
> *Example for discussion, not a guarantee:* a session of ~25 kids at ~$17.50/seat is ~$437 in ticket sales → venue share ~$130. Real earnings depend on price and fill. `[PROOF — replace with real session numbers once available.]`

**Closing line:** No upfront cost to you. You earn when families show up.

**Speaker notes:** Be honest — don't quote monthly income they can't bank on. The 40/30/30 split is the real, fixed model (`pricing/monetization-model.md`); the rupee/dollar figure depends on real fill.

---

## Slide 7 — We bring the hard parts

**Headline:** You don't run it. We do.

**Body:**
- **Teachers:** vetted, sourced locally near your venue.
- **Curriculum & materials:** reviewed, age-appropriate projects and kits, ready to run.
- **Operations:** a student **ambassador** handles setup, check-in, and flow on the day.
- **Marketing & booking:** we fill the seats (landing page, Telegram, local social). You just give us a head start — a quick note to the parents who already come to your center, since a warm intro converts far better than a cold ad.

**Speaker notes:** Be clear on the division of labor so the venue doesn't think filling the room is their job. *Learnish* runs acquisition; the venue's only "marketing" ask is an optional warm intro to its existing families — that's the accelerant, not the engine. The ambassador model means the venue carries almost no operational burden.

---

## Slide 8 — Safe and trusted, together

**Headline:** Your reputation is on the line too. So safety is the first thing we build.

**Body:**
- Every project is **reviewed and age-appropriate** (5–17).
- Teachers are **vetted**; parents are welcome to stay in the room.
- Clear safeguarding expectations for the venue and our staff. `[PROOF — link real safeguarding policy.]`

**Closing line:** We protect the trust you've built. That's the deal.

**Speaker notes:** This is the partner's biggest unspoken worry. Address it head-on, plainly, no marketing gloss (voice guide: safety tone).

---

## Slide 9 — How we start (a single pilot weekend)

**Headline:** One workshop. One weekend. Then we both decide.

**Body:**
1. Pick a date and a project that fits the families at your center.
2. We bring the teacher, materials, and an ambassador, and we market the session; you open the space and send a warm note to your existing families.
3. We debrief together: did the families love it? Do we run a regular slot?

**Speaker notes:** Low commitment is the whole pitch. One session, see how it goes.

---

## Slide 10 — Let's run one workshop

**Headline:** Pick a weekend. We'll handle the rest.

**CTA box:**
> **Let's schedule a pilot workshop at [venue].**
> [name · role · phone / Telegram]

**Speaker notes:** Ask for a specific date before you leave. A named contact, not a generic line.

## Appendix A1 — The Learnish Club (the bigger picture)

**Use when:** The partner asks "what's Learnish beyond the workshop?"
Learnish is a safe, trusted kids' L&D platform (5–17): a free app + courses, a STEAM toys store (`learnish.uz`), kits, and workshops — wrapped as the **Learnish Club**. Workshops are the in-person trust + acquisition engine. Partnering on workshops connects your families to the whole ecosystem. `[Pricing: intro, being validated — pricing/monetization-model.md.]`

## Appendix A2 — Ambassador model

**Use when:** Recruiting a student to run ops, or explaining who's on the ground.
Student **ambassadors** run workshop logistics, comped with benefits (membership / store credits / experience), not payroll — keeping the model lean and doubling as community-building. Adapt this deck for campus ambassador recruiting: lead with mission + experience, not pay.

## Appendix A3 — FAQ

- **Does it cost the venue anything?** No upfront cost. You earn 30% of seats; we carry teacher + ops.
- **Who's responsible for safety?** Shared — reviewed content + vetted teachers from us; a safe space + safeguarding cooperation from you. `[PROOF — policy link.]`
- **What if a session doesn't fill?** We debrief and adjust topic/timing/marketing; the pilot is exactly to test fit.
- **Can we co-brand?** `[to-decide — keep Learnish brand primary for trust consistency; discuss case by case.]`
- **What languages?** Lead in your district's language; we support UZ/EN/RU (`brand-voice/language-matrix.md`).
