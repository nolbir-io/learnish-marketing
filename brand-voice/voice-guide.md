# Voice Guide

How Learnish sounds across every email, landing page, workshop flyer, app screen, and social post. This is the source-of-truth voice doc; every other folder borrows from it. Built from the [product marketing context](../.agents/product-marketing-context.md) and [operating principles](../docs/strategy/operating-principles.md).

## The one thing to remember

**We speak to two audiences at once: parents (who buy) and kids 5–17 (who use).** Trust is for the parent; delight is for the kid. Every piece of copy should make the parent feel *safe and smart* and make the kid feel *excited to do it*. When the two pull apart, **trust wins** — a parent who doesn't trust us never lets the kid in the door.

## Personality

Learnish is the caring, capable friend who runs the best weekend program in the neighborhood. Warm, never cold. Confident, never hype. Fun, never silly about safety.

Strategic analogs (see [operating principles](../docs/strategy/operating-principles.md)):

- **LEGO** — craft, hands-on making, "look what you built," quietly trusted by parents.
- **Duolingo** — playful delight and habit, a friendly character energy, learning that doesn't feel like a chore.
- **Disney** — family trust and wonder; safe is the foundation everything else stands on.

### Mascot — Nish, the Learnish bee

Learnish has a **bee mascot named Nish** (working name — "Lear‑*nish*," so saying the character's name says the brand). Nish carries the Duolingo-style "friendly character energy" and is primarily a **kid-facing** device: the guide who invites kids to build, make, and come to workshops.

- **Nish speaks to kids, not parents.** In parent-facing trust copy (safety, pricing, data), keep Nish in the background — warmth comes from the bee, but trust comes from real teachers and reviewed content.
- **Bee logic, used lightly:** curiosity, busy-in-a-good-way, making something sweet together (the "hive"/community), gathering knowledge. Avoid stinging/pest framing.
- **Consistent across languages:** the name *Nish* stays the same in UZ/EN/RU; only Nish's lines are localized.

## Voice principles

- **Reassuring over clever.** A parent deciding what their child does with their time wants calm confidence, not wordplay. Lead with safety and clarity.
- **Concrete over abstract.** Show real kids, real workshops, real things made — not stock claims or "transform your child's future." Name the kit, the language, the Saturday.
- **Exciting, not hyped.** Genuine enthusiasm ("kids built a working robot hand") beats superlatives ("revolutionary learning platform"). Let the thing be exciting; don't claim it.
- **Family-first, value-honest.** Talk to the whole household and be straight about money. Parents are weighing scattered apps, toys, and tutoring — show the value plainly, don't oversell.
- **Local and human.** Uzbek/Russian context, local teachers, local venues, real names of places. We're the neighbors, not a global app translated into the region.

## What we lead with

Two anchors that every asset should reinforce:

- **Safe & trusted = reviewed, age-appropriate content.** This is the single proof we lead with on safety (OQ-003). When we say "safe," we mean *every course, kit, and session is reviewed and age-appropriate for 5–17* — provable, not a vague promise. Vetted teachers and parents-in-the-room are supporting proof, but reviewed content is the headline.
- **Messaging hero = STEAM toys + weekend workshops.** Lead brand messaging with the two **tangible, hands-on** offers kids and parents can picture — the STEAM toys and the weekend workshops — not the abstract membership (OQ-013). The **Learnish Club** is the wrapper that bundles them and the free app/courses are the on-ramp; but the hook is "real things to build and a real session to come to."

## Tone by audience

| Audience | Feel | Sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| **Parents** (buyers) | Safe, smart, supported | "Vetted teachers, content you'd approve of, and other parents in the room. Your first workshop is free." |
| **Kids 5–12** (users) | Fun, playful, "I get to make stuff" | "Build a volcano that actually erupts. Bring a friend." |
| **Teens 13–17** (users) | Respected, real, not babyish | "Real Python, real Spanish, real money skills — with people your age, not a cartoon." |
| **Ambassadors / partners** | Trusted collaborator, mission-driven | "Help run the best weekend program in your district. We bring the playbook; you bring the community." |

## Tone by moment

- **Acquisition (workshop invites, ads, social):** warm + exciting, low pressure. The ask is "come to a free workshop," not "buy now."
- **Onboarding / activation (app, welcome emails):** encouraging and simple. Celebrate first steps; reduce anxiety ("you don't have to figure it all out today").
- **Monetization (Club upgrade, pricing):** confident and value-clear. State what's included and what it costs without flinching.
- **Safety / sensitive (data, kids' wellbeing):** plain, specific, no marketing gloss. Say exactly what we do and don't do.

## Message structure (default)

1. **The tension, named gently** — the screen-time guilt, the scattered options.
2. **What Learnish does** — concrete, the relevant slice (app / workshop / Club / kit).
3. **Why it's safe and worth it** — proof: real teachers, real sessions, real value.
4. **One clear next step** — usually *come to a free workshop* or *create a free family account*.

## Words to use

safe, trusted, exciting, learning, growth, hands-on, build/make, community, family, for kids 5–17, real-world, weekend workshop, vetted teachers, the Learnish Club.

## Words to avoid

- **Hype:** revolutionary, best-in-class, game-changer, world-class, cutting-edge, unlock your child's potential.
- **Cold/jargon:** edutainment, solution, leverage, engagement (as in metrics), users (say *kids* or *families*).
- **Unsafe-sounding or over-promising:** addictive (except naming the problem), guaranteed results, "make your child a genius," anything implying outcomes we can't back.
- **Screen-time framing** except when naming the problem we solve — never position Learnish as "more screen time."
- **Merchant/e-commerce holdovers** from the Own fork: merchant, seller, GMV, marketplace OS, launch plan call.

## Naming and capitalization

- **Learnish** — the company/brand.
- **the Learnish Club** — the membership framing (capital C). Tiers: **Digital Club**, **Full Club**.
- **family account** — lowercase; what a parent creates (our north-star = signups).
- **workshop** — lowercase; the in-person weekend session.
- **kit** — lowercase; the STEAM/learning kit shipped to Full Club members.
- **ambassador** — lowercase; a student running on-the-ground workshop ops.

See the [product context glossary](../.agents/product-marketing-context.md) for definitions; keep these exact across every asset.

## Examples — on voice vs. off voice

**Hero headline**

- Off: "The revolutionary all-in-one EdTech platform unlocking every child's potential."
- On: "Make screen time worth it. Safe, exciting learning for kids 5–17 — starting with a free weekend workshop."

**Workshop invite (to parents)**

- Off: "Sign up now for our premium STEAM enrichment experience!"
- On: "This Saturday in Yunusobod: kids build a working robot hand with a vetted teacher, while you meet other parents. First one's free."

**Kid-facing app copy (ages 5–12)**

- Off: "Engage with our gamified learning modules to maximize outcomes."
- On: "Pick a quest. Build something real. Show your family what you made."

**Pricing line**

- Off: "Incredible value — get everything for one low price!"
- On: "The Full Club bundles ~$280+ of kits, workshops, and courses for visibly less — and you can start free."

## Founder vs. company voice

Learnish speaks as a **company with a warm human face**. In the early founder-led phases (see [playbook](../docs/playbook/README.md)), founder-signed posts and workshop invites are encouraged for trust — but the brand voice above holds whether the byline is the founder, an ambassador, or Learnish itself.
