## Product Overview
**One-liner:** Learnish is a safe, trusted learning & development platform that makes everyday learning exciting for kids aged 5–17.

**What it does:** Learnish blends four things into one membership — the **Learnish Club**: a freemium app and courses (languages, STEAM, personal finance), a STEAM toys store, and in-person, teacher-led weekend workshops. Parents are the buyers; kids 5–17 are the end users. The free app and courses drive the north-star (**family account signups**); the Club, store, and workshops monetize. The motion is community / word-of-mouth led, anchored by in-person weekend workshops that make the "safe and trusted" promise tangible.

**Product category:** B2C kids/family EdTech — multi-modal (digital learning + physical kits + in-person workshops + commerce).

**Product type:** Consumer subscription (membership) + e-commerce + workshop marketplace.

**Business model:** Blended, framed as the Learnish Club. Free tier (app + freemium courses) → **Digital Club** (~$5–8/mo per family, ~100% margin) → **Full Club** (per child: Solo ~$18/mo, Family/3 ~$40/mo, Big Family/5 ~$60/mo, 5+ contact us; includes 4 kits/yr + ≥4 workshops/yr + 2× store credits). Additional lines: STEAM toys store and a weekend workshop marketplace (platform 40% / teacher 30% / venue 30%). Annual prepay is the focus. Full detail in [`pricing/monetization-model.md`](../pricing/monetization-model.md).

## Target Audience
**Primary buyer:** Parents of kids aged 5–17 in Uzbekistan / Central Asia who want their kids to achieve their best — and who feel the daily tension between passive screen time and real growth.

**End users:** Kids 5–17 (the experience must delight them or parents won't renew).

**Geographic focus:** Uzbekistan / Central Asia. Tashkent first, then regional cities.

**Languages:** Uzbek + Russian (with English in the product). Working assumption: UZ + RU in parallel for first-touch.

**Primary use case:** Give kids exciting, safe, productive things to do — learning courses, hands-on STEAM kits, and weekend workshops — instead of unproductive screen time, while parents get trust, convenience, and a community of other families.

**Jobs to be done:**
- Replace passive screen time with something that grows my child (and that my child actually wants to do).
- Find safe, trusted, age-appropriate learning I don't have to vet from scratch.
- Give my child real-world experiences and friendships, not just another app.
- Do it affordably for the whole family, without juggling separate apps, toy stores, and tutoring centers.

**Use cases:**
- Parent downloads the free app / creates a family account (north-star), kids use courses and the app.
- Family attends a weekend workshop; kids build something and make friends; parents meet other parents.
- Family upgrades to Digital Club, then Full Club (kits + workshops shipped/scheduled across the year).
- Parent buys STEAM toys à la carte or with Club store credits.

## Problems & Pain Points
**Core problem:** Kids default to passive, addictive screen time (YouTube, TikTok, Roblox, Netflix), and parents lack a single safe, trusted, exciting alternative that actually competes for the child's attention.

**Why alternatives fall short:**
- **Attention apps** (YouTube/YouTube Kids, TikTok, Instagram, Netflix, Roblox, Facebook) win the time but don't grow the child — and parents distrust them.
- **Global EdTech apps** (Khan Academy Kids, Duolingo/Duolingo ABC, ABCmouse) are one more app fighting the algorithm, with no local presence, no in-person trust, and limited local-language/context fit.
- **Kits** (KiwiCo, MEL Science) are great but expensive, import-dependent, and not localized.
- **Workshops / tutoring** (Outschool globally; local tutoring centers and kids clubs) are fragmented, inconsistent, and not bundled with digital or commerce.
- **Marketplaces** (e.g. Uzum) sell STEAM toys but offer no learning, curation, or community.

**What it costs them:** Lost developmental time, guilt over screen time, money spent on scattered apps/toys/tutoring with no coherent result, and no community of like-minded families.

**Emotional tension:** Guilt and worry about screen time; fear of unsafe or low-quality content; desire to give their child the best but uncertainty about what's actually good and worth paying for.

## Competitive Landscape
Split into two frames (see [`competitive-analysis/`](../competitive-analysis/)):

**Attention competitors (compete for the kid's time):** YouTube / YouTube Kids, TikTok, Instagram, Netflix, Roblox, Facebook. They frame the problem and the emotional tension — they are the default we displace.

**Category competitors (compete for the parent's spend):** Khan Academy Kids, Duolingo / Duolingo ABC, ABCmouse, KiwiCo / MEL Science (kits), Outschool (workshops), local tutoring centers & kids clubs, marketplaces selling STEAM toys (e.g. Uzum). They frame the alternatives a parent weighs.

**Our edge over both:** Local, in-person trust and community (real workshops, vetted local teachers, parents who meet) + a multi-modal Club that no single competitor offers — something a global app or a local marketplace structurally cannot replicate here.

## Differentiation
**Key differentiators:**
- **Multi-modal Club** — app + courses + kits + workshops in one membership, not a single product.
- **In-person community** — weekend workshops as the trust and acquisition engine; the "safe and trusted" promise made tangible.
- **Local depth** — UZ/RU language and context, local teachers, local venues, local payments/fulfillment.
- **Family-first economics** — generous free/digital layer per family; physical priced per child to protect margin.

**How we do it differently:** Lead with in-person workshops that build trust and word of mouth, then convert attending families into free signups and Club members — instead of buying attention with ads.

**Why that's better:** Trust and community compound and can't be copied by an algorithm; the blended Club captures more of a family's "learning + play + commerce" spend than any single competitor.

**Why customers choose us:** [to-validate] Safe and trusted, genuinely exciting for the kid, all-in-one and good value, and backed by a real local community.

## Objections
| Objection | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| "My kid will just go back to YouTube/Roblox." | Learnish is designed to be exciting (games, characters, kits, real workshops), not a chore — it competes for attention, not just intention. |
| "Is it safe and appropriate?" | Vetted teachers, reviewed content, age-appropriate design, and a clear guarantee — safety is the core promise, not a footnote. |
| "It's expensive." | The Club bundles ~$280+ of à la carte value (kits + workshops + courses + app) for visibly less; a free tier and a cheap Digital Club lower the entry. |
| "Another subscription I'll forget." | Weekend workshops, shipped kits, and a family ritual keep it active; annual prepay removes the monthly "should we cancel" moment. |
| "Is this just for little kids?" | Tiered for 5–17 — languages, real STEAM, and personal finance for teens, not just early learning. |

**Anti-persona:** Parents who only want free passive entertainment and won't engage with learning or community; families with no interest in any paid tier or in-person participation.

## Switching Dynamics
**Push:** Guilt about screen time; scattered, unvetted, expensive alternatives; no community.

**Pull:** A safe, exciting, all-in-one Club; real workshops and friendships; local trust and language fit; clear value vs. à la carte.

**Habit:** Defaulting to YouTube/Roblox/TikTok; ad-hoc toy purchases and one-off tutoring.

**Anxiety:** Will my kid actually use it? Is it safe? Is it worth the money? Will it just be another ignored app?
